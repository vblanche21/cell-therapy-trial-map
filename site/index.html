How the data was built

  • Sources. ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, interventional studies; searches for CAR-T and TCR-T terms and product names, plus in vivo products) and the WHO ICTRP search portal, which gathers the WHO primary registries: ChiCTR, EU CTIS and EU CTR, jRCT, CTRI, ANZCTR, CRIS, DRKS, ISRCTN, IRCT, ReBEC, TCTR, PACTR and others.
  • Duplicates. The same trial often sits in several registries. ICTRP copies of ClinicalTrials.gov records were dropped; other records were linked through shared IDs (NCT or EudraCT numbers) or identical titles, then through a similarity check (same country, start within about a year, same cell type, and the same product code, or close sponsor and title words).
  • Classes. Rules on titles, interventions and conditions sort each trial into CAR-T or TCR-T, in vivo or ex vivo, and cancer, autoimmune and immune-mediated, or other. In vivo trials were checked by hand. CAR-NK, CAR-macrophage and TIL trials are left out, as are supportive-care studies around CAR-T (rehabilitation, imaging, drugs to prevent or treat CRS and ICANS, infections after infusion).
  • Running years. A trial counts in a year if it started by the end of that year and had not ended before it. The end is the completion date; ongoing ClinicalTrials.gov trials run until the data date; records in other registries without a completion date get the median length of completed ClinicalTrials.gov trials of the same phase. Withdrawn and not-yet-recruiting trials are left out.
  • Places. A trial counts once in each country where it lists a site, and once in each region. Country names follow Natural Earth, so Hong Kong and Taiwan appear apart from China. Map projection: Equal Earth.

Limits

  • Counts are trials, not patients, and say nothing about trial size or results.
  • Trials registered only on China's drug agency platform (CDE) are missing, because it is not part of the WHO network. No trial in these registries lists a site in Africa.
  • Estimated end dates (mostly ChiCTR) can make a trial count for too long or too short. ClinicalTrials.gov records with an "unknown" status, not updated for years, stop counting at their old end date even if the trial goes on.
  • Some duplicates remain, mostly among Chinese records, and rules can misclassify a few trials. Corrections are welcome.
